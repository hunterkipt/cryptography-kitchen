The Wedding!

The wedding will be held at Gwangju Hyan-gyo (광주향교) Gwangju Hyan-gyo is a Confucianist school that holds all sorts of events. From Visit Korea:

Hyanggyo was a national educational institution during the Joseon dynasty, where the tablets of distinguished Confucian scholars were enshrined, and rituals were held in their honor.
In spring and fall, it hosts Seokjeon, a ritual honoring Confucius, along with Seodang (traditional Confucian school) experiences and traditional weddings, offering opportunities to immerse in Confucian culture.
In summer, crape myrtle flowers bloom, and in fall, the old ginkgo tree turns golden yellow, beautifully contrasting with the traditional Joseon-style hanok architecture.

Tour a Buddhist Temple!

Following the wedding, take some time to rest and recharge! We will be heading to a local Bhuddist temple, Mugaksa (무각사). Do not worry about getting here on time! Hearan and I are able to change out of our wedding clothes at the venue, so we will head right over. If you want to take time to head back to your hotel, change, and rest feel free to do so! Touring the temple in total will take approximately 30 minutes. We plan to head to a nearby cafe or go for a nice walk once eveyone has seen the temple. From Tour Gwangju:

Originally located in Yeouisan Mountain, Mugaksa Temple started out as a religious place for combat soldiers at Sangmudae, or the Army Warfare Military Academy. While the academy may have moved, the temple site remained and the entire mountain was designated the May 18 Memorial Exhibit Hall.
Currently located in Sangmu District, the heart of Gwangju's largest financial and administrative district, Mugaksa Temple is an urban temple comparable to Bongeunsa Temple in Seoul. The temple's Book Cafe, Lotus Gallery, and traditional tea salon Sarangchae have not only broken down the wall of religion but also become places of culture, art, meetings, and relaxation for citizens.
Every Saturday, the temple organizes Bomulseom, or "Treasure Island", which is a flea market for people to buy and sell recycled goods. All combined, the temple is a destination for visitors and an on-site educational center for students.
